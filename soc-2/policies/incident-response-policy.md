# Incident Response Policy

**Document ID:** SOC2-POL-003  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft — Portfolio Example  
**Policy Owner:** IT Security  
**Review Frequency:** Annually and after significant incidents

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for identifying, reporting, assessing, responding to, and recovering from information security incidents.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, contractors, and other authorized users who access organizational information, systems, networks, and services.

## 3. Policy Requirements

### 3.1 Incident Identification and Reporting
- Suspected or confirmed security incidents must be reported promptly through designated reporting channels.
- Users must report suspicious activity, unauthorized access, data exposure, malware, and suspected service disruptions.
- Reports must include available details such as the time, affected systems, observed activity, and potential impact.

### 3.2 Incident Classification and Assessment
- Incidents must be recorded in an incident register or approved tracking system.
- Each incident must be assessed for severity, scope, business impact, and potential data exposure.
- Incidents must be prioritized according to defined severity criteria.
- Escalation must occur when an incident exceeds the authority or capabilities of the initial response team.

### 3.3 Roles and Responsibilities
- **Incident Response Lead:** Coordinates response activities and assigns responsibilities.
- **IT Security:** Investigates incidents, analyzes evidence, and coordinates containment.
- **IT Operations:** Supports isolation, remediation, restoration, and technical recovery.
- **Management:** Provides oversight and approves significant business decisions.
- **Legal and Privacy Representatives:** Assess applicable legal, contractual, and privacy obligations.
- **Users:** Report incidents promptly and follow response instructions.

### 3.4 Containment
- Appropriate measures must be taken to limit incident impact and prevent further unauthorized activity.
- Containment actions must consider business continuity and evidence preservation.
- Significant containment decisions must be documented.

### 3.5 Investigation and Evidence Handling
- Relevant logs, system records, and other evidence must be preserved where feasible.
- Evidence must be protected against unauthorized access, alteration, or loss.
- Investigation activities, findings, and decisions must be documented.
- Access to incident evidence must be limited to authorized personnel.

### 3.6 Eradication and Recovery
- The incident response team must identify and address the underlying cause where feasible.
- Affected systems must be restored using approved recovery procedures.
- Systems must be checked for security risks before returning to normal operation.
- Recovery activities and validation results must be documented.

### 3.7 Communication and Notification
- Internal incident communications must be coordinated through authorized personnel.
- External notifications must be reviewed and approved by appropriate representatives.
- Applicable contractual, legal, and regulatory notification requirements must be assessed.
- Communications must be accurate and limited to authorized information.

### 3.8 Post-Incident Review
- Significant incidents must receive a post-incident review.
- Reviews must identify root causes, response effectiveness, and lessons learned.
- Corrective actions must be assigned owners and target dates.
- Incident records must be retained according to applicable retention requirements.

### 3.9 Testing and Preparedness
- Incident response procedures must be tested periodically through exercises or simulations.
- Relevant personnel must receive appropriate incident response awareness and training.
- Lessons from exercises and actual incidents must be used to improve response procedures.

## 4. Incident Documentation

Incident records should include:
- Incident identifier and date reported
- Incident description and affected systems
- Severity and impact assessment
- Response actions and timeline
- Evidence references and investigation findings
- Communication and notification decisions
- Recovery details and closure approval
- Corrective actions, owners, and due dates

## 5. Exceptions

Exceptions must be documented, justified, assessed for risk, and approved by an authorized owner. Compensating measures must be considered where appropriate.

## 6. Compliance and Review

Violations must be reported and handled under applicable organizational procedures. This policy must be reviewed annually and after significant incidents or organizational changes.
